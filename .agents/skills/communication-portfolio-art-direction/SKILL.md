# Communication Portfolio — Art Direction

## Core brief

Treat this as a magazine art director's assignment for an applicant, not a corporate template. The applicant's actual creative practice defines the look. Read project `DESIGN_REFERENCE.md` and any genuine applicant résumé, photos, case study assets and statements.

## Step 1 — Inspect, distinguish, avoid attribution

- Read source PDF art references and identify *visual patterns only*: contrast, crop, recurring motifs, hierarchy, grids, typography, texture, pacing, photo treatment and image-to-text ratio.
- Separate inspiration from applicant evidence. Do not import the references' photographs, portraits, project achievements, copy or branding into the student's site.
- Inspect the applicant's own images/activities to infer genuine visual vocabulary (e.g., journalism/print, filmmaking, dance, fashion, photography, street culture, activism, education, science communication).

## Step 2 — Art-direction rationale before coding

Write an in-repo concise `ART_DIRECTION.md` with:
1. A 5–10 word original concept/tagline.
2. Audience and 30-second scan goals (US admissions officers, not designers only).
3. Three emotional adjectives, two anti-goals.
4. Typography pairings and allowed handwritten accent usage.
5. Color tokens, text contrast, section rhythms.
6. Imagery treatment, cropping and placement rules.
7. Motif rule: a mark appears because it relates to the student or the subject, not as filler.
8. Motion plan, mobile simplification and reduced-motion plan.

## Step 3 — Interpret the three references

Default blended direction: Lê Hoàng Bách's expressive portrait and story chapters; Phan Nguyễn Linh Dung's passionate red-black-white editorial photography; Nguyễn Phúc Sơn's street-culture collage and authentic project proof. This is a **reference synthesis**, never a mandate to clone one of the PDFs.

Suggested optional base tokens:
- Paper #F4F0E8; ink #181818; crimson #BD172C; orange #EE603A; teal #28535A.
- Use large tightly tracked editorial display headlines, body text with generous line-height, selective handwritten annotations and handdrawn border/shapes.
- Set up desktop asymmetrical art-directed compositions and intuitive single-column mobile versions.

## Step 4 — Three design modes

Produce three internal options before implementation, then choose one and document why:
- Editorial Protest: bold type, documentary imagery, crimson with print texture.
- Kinetic Magazine: oversized grid-breaking headers, kinetic text, typographic chapter transitions.
- Minimal Punk: largely calm off-white pages, striking black type and one controlled raw accent.

Pick the mode that makes *the specific student* more credible and memorable, not the loudest one.

## Step 5 — Practical visual constraints

- Limit font families to 2–3 functional roles and variable weights when possible.
- Maintain reliable heading/body contrast and never put long text over busy photography.
- Make case-study cards unlike generic cards; integrate grid, typographic numbering, imagery, modest overlays and accessible links.
- Texture in moderation, especially mobile; avoid fake raster noise over paragraphs.
- No autoplay audio, forced cursor takeover, long preloader or full-screen animation gating main content.
- Run a visual pass at 1440px, 768px, 390px and 320px. No clipped copy or invisible nav.

## Completion

Deliver a compact art-direction decision log and corresponding theme tokens / component specifications. If source images are missing, create an asset manifest with clearly labeled placeholders rather than fabricated photography.
