# Admissions Storytelling — Communication/Media

## Goal

Help a US admissions reader recognize the applicant's intellectual curiosity, communication skill, initiative, leadership, creative judgment and community impact within a short skim. Use the student's *actual* facts as the only source for biographical claims.

## Source policy

- The three design-reference PDFs belong to other students. Never borrow their names, project work, metrics, awards or story arcs for the applicant.
- Read available résumé, self-description, project assets, website links and original media. Create `CONTENT_INVENTORY.md` listing source, claim, media, permission/ownership, and verification status for each candidate item.
- Separate facts from interpretation; say `not provided` when a detail is absent. Never infer awards or measured impact from mere photos.
- Do not create fake testimonial quotes, media publication logos, school names or third-party endorsements.

## Editorial selection

Choose 3–5 featured projects based on **substance**, not aesthetics alone. Score (0–3 each): applicant agency, creative/strategic decisions, tangible outputs, evidence, measurable/reasonable impact and distinctive voice. Show this rubric in working notes, not necessarily on the public website.

Prioritize:
- editorial writing / reporting / documentary / podcast / filmmaking;
- campaigns / social content / brand storytelling / PR / advocacy;
- digital media / visual identity / photography / design;
- leadership in communication-oriented school/community projects.

Put generic volunteering/certificates in a lower-priority section unless they show communication methodology and concrete work.

## Copy framework

**One-line positioning:** `I use [format/media/skill] to [tell/translate/amplify] [specific subject or audience concern]`. This is a structure, NOT text to invent for the student.

**Homepage hero:** applicant name; truthful discipline/intention; 8–18-word viewpoint; direct Work link. Avoid inflated claims (visionary, game-changing, world's first).

**Each project:**
1. What it is, when, and applicant's exact role.
2. Audience and communication problem/opportunity.
3. Key insight, strategic decisions and why chosen.
4. Specific media outputs (posts, podcast, film, publication, photo essay, website, workshop etc.).
5. Authentic evidence (assets, URLs, screenshots, credits) and verified results with context.
6. Reflection: a concrete learning or changed assumption if source supports it.

**Microcopy:** 20–35-word home previews, 50–90-word project summaries, body case study ~200–400 words where sources support them; favor clear active voice, 1 idea per sentence.

## Admissions ethics and provenance

- Preserve collaborator credit and distinguish personal role from team achievement.
- Never convert aspirations/next-phase plans into accomplished results.
- When a result is unverified, describe activities, not unverified outcomes.
- Keep official résumé/certificate access optional and contextual; no ungated display of sensitive student details.
- Write in precise English for an international admissions audience, keeping cultural references understandable without flattening them.
- If no student material is provided, produce content schema and `[Student content required]` placeholders; do not populate invented achievements.

## Outputs

`CONTENT_INVENTORY.md`, suggested information hierarchy, homepage microcopy, 3–5 consistent project summaries, an evidence/missing-info checklist, and valid structured content (`data/projects.ts` or equivalent) if asked to implement.
