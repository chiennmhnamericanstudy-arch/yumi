# Heritage in motion — giving tradition a new voice

Audience: US university admissions readers. In 30 seconds: identify Như Ngọc as a high-school student interested in Communication; understand her founder/designer roles; find factual evidence and contact.

Subject inventory: Hát Bội mask workshop; Cổ Sắc Tân Thanh; cobalt character outlines; Yumi keychains; Moving, moving... comic; debate microphone; Hàm Cần classroom; Disabled performance; lotus conical hat; Ho Chi Minh City; Le Hong Phong school.

Idea: Ngọc’s movement between cultural storytelling, character illustration and spoken dialogue becomes a visual magazine with five distinct chapters. The opening cover sets the cultural process photograph against a vivid original Yumi character, with her name as the masthead.

Internal options: Editorial Protest (too confrontational for the evidence), Kinetic Magazine (selected for distinct cultural and visual chapters), Minimal Punk (too sparse for the required case studies). Taste draw: type-only-statement, seed 1790643908; initially chosen when no media had publication approval. After approval of the selected photos and artwork, the type-only cover evolved into an image-led dark cover. Retain brand palette and Syne/DM Sans. No unrelated stock pictures. The user’s restrained-motion requirements override ornate motion prescriptions in optional design guidance.

Emotional goals: curious, expressive, thoughtful. Anti-goals: agency marketing and inflated achievement wall.

Typography: locally bundled Syne display with Vietnamese subset and DM Sans body. Five main scale levels with fluid display sizes. No fake handwritten student marks. Paper #F7F2EA, ink #17171C, red #D94537, cobalt #344BC4, blush #F4D7DE, mint #DDECE5 and gold #DFAE56. Red is a large surface, with ink text where white would fail small-text contrast.

Composition: enormous name across a near-black cover, with approved Hát Bội masks and Yumi portrait arranged as adjacent editorial panels. A direct identity line sits above and a work CTA below. The red heritage chapter pairs process imagery with oversized text and a quiet ghost title. The cobalt studio spread layers a character sheet over a primary portrait and uses its own oversized background lettering. Supporting projects become broad photographic spreads with alternating image placement; the work index uses asymmetric project posters. The Yumi case gallery uses a three-column editorial flow on desktop. The About page makes a two-image collage from approved artwork and cultural material. Mobile becomes a deliberate single column with uncropped project-image previews. Any missing future media falls back to a clearly labeled text-only plate.

Live interaction: grouped Yumi archive with keyboard-operable dialog and previous/next navigation. Following two explicit user confirmations on 28 October 2026, 14 selected works replace the gallery placeholders. Portraits, character expressions, posters, a comic and two keychain images retain their full compositions. Seven documentary photographs support the other projects, with factual captions and no inferred face identity. The homepage reuses approved masks and Yumi artwork as an intentional cover, then pairs its two featured image moments with three further approved project photographs; all five projects have images in the work index.

Motion: one modest wordmark entrance; staggered viewport reveals; project-link feedback. No essential content hidden without JavaScript, no autoplay, no scroll trapping. Reduced motion disables travel and smooth scroll.

The three user-provided reference PDFs were fetched, all 81 pages visually reviewed and their design principles compared in `docs/DESIGN_REFERENCE_REVIEW.md`. No reference imagery, copy, personal facts or exact layout is used.

Page argument: identity and Communication focus → cultural initiative proved by résumé-supported role and team outputs → illustration practice proved by the private portfolio → debate/community/film broaden the media → education and interests situate the student → verified email enables contact.
